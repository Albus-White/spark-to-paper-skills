# Idea Discovery Contract

The lifecycle preserves four distinct objects:

1. `Idea seed`: the user's literal starting point and constraints;
2. `Idea candidates`: evidence-grounded alternatives, including faithful keep/narrow options;
3. `Idea selection`: one model-authored comparison and decision;
4. `active Idea`: the selected candidate copied exactly into lifecycle state.

Only the active Idea and its versioned descendants may own claims or contracts. Candidate sets,
Paper Wiki gaps, embeddings, clusters, and novelty retrieval reports are discovery evidence, not
parallel truth.

Candidate discovery reads two different calibrations. The science profile supplies closest work,
accepted paradigms, benchmark/evaluator practice, and field writing conventions. The venue profile
supplies target-publication constraints and empirical scale. Do not use venue averages to invent a
scientific mechanism, and do not use leading-field prose as the target venue template.

Every candidate states why it may fail. Selection weighs falsifiability, scientific value, closest-work
difference, feasibility, resource fit, and risk of false novelty. No scalar score, embedding threshold,
or required recombination pattern decides the winner.

After selection, new memory or test evidence may motivate a versioned Idea change. It never edits the
active Idea in place. Changes follow L0-L4 invalidation and independent-confirmation rules.
